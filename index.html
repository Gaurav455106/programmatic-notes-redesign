In 30 seconds

  • Programmatic = buying and selling ad space on the open internet with an automated system. It is not coding.
  • Meta and Google only sell their own space (they are walled gardens). Programmatic reaches everything else: news sites, apps, OTT, audio, connected TV, digital billboards.
  • Every impression is auctioned in real time. This is called RTB (real-time bidding).

The story in four eras: Zara wants to run ads

1980s · manual

Zara calls Newspaper 1, Newspaper 2, a billboard owner and an FM station one by one: "What's your price?"

Problem: completely manual, one deal at a time.

Ad networks

Someone bundles 10 newspapers (later, thousands of websites) and sells them all to Zara from one place.

Problem: price discrepancy. Avinash.com sells the same slot at ₹150 CPM on one network and ₹400+ on another, so Zara can pay twice, at two prices, for the same space.

Internet + walled gardens

Meta and Google build their own systems: "Give me budget, audience and creative, I'll show the ad."

Problem: they only sell their own inventory (Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, GDN). You cannot buy a Times of India homepage takeover or a Disney+ Hotstar slot there.

Programmatic

One automated system for the open internet. Buyers and sellers meet, and every impression is auctioned in milliseconds.

How big is the gap?

About 1.34 billion websites exist; Google Display Network covers roughly 2 million. Cyrus's rule of thumb: Meta + Google ≈ 20% of time spent online; the open internet is the other ~80%.

What lives in that 80%

  • News and publisher sites outside GDN
  • Apps and games: Zomato, Swiggy, CRED, Uber
  • OTT: Hotstar, Prime Video, JioCinema
  • Audio: Spotify, podcasts
  • CTV (LG, Samsung, Google TV) and DOOH screens
Three benefits to say in the viva

Freedom to run ads anywhere · Scale from 20% of the internet to 80% · Choice of DSP, exchange, SSP, data source, and auction or no auction.

In 30 seconds

  • Buyer uses a DSP, seller uses an SSP, they meet at the Ad Exchange, and the price is set by real-time bidding.
  • Flow: Advertiser → DSP → Ad Exchange → SSP → Publisher.
  • A DMP is not in that line. It feeds audience data into the DSP.

Who sets the floor?

Hotstar won't sell below ₹250 CPM. That minimum is set by the publisher in its SSP.

Anyone can be a publisher

A role, not a company type. Swiggy and Zerodha's learning pages become publishers when they sell ad slots. Times of India becomes an advertiser when it promotes its own magazine. Flipkart's ad on Hotstar: Hotstar is the publisher.

Ad network vs ad exchange

Network = a wholesaler that aggregates many publishers' inventory and resells it. Exchange = the open marketplace where each impression is auctioned between DSPs and SSPs.

Meta vs programmatic (viva favourite)

Social and search wait for the user. If Khushi never opens Instagram, a Meta ad can't reach her. A search ad needs her to search first.

Programmatic follows the user across the open internet: the news site she reads, the game she opens, YouTube, her smart TV, a digital billboard on her way home. That is the scale argument.

In 30 seconds

  • Every time a page loads, its ad slot is auctioned in about 100 milliseconds: bid request → DSPs bid → winner's ad loads with the page.
  • An impression counts when the ad loads. A viewable impression needs ≥50% of the ad on screen for 1 s (display) or 2 s (video).
  • Most exchanges ran second-price auctions; with first-price, bid shading stops you overpaying.

Who picks the model? The exchange, following the publisher's (or reselling network's) instructions. You can't choose it in the DSP.

Header bidding

A publisher-side technology. Instead of offering a slot to one SSP at a time, the page offers it to several SSPs at once (Google, Yahoo, PubMatic) and takes the best price. More competition → CPMs go up.

Win rate

Auctions won ÷ auctions entered. 450 wins from 1,000 auctions = 45%. You only worry about it when the campaign underspends; then a low win rate says "raise the bid".

"Which DSP should we use?"

There's no single answer. Decide on:

  • The channels / inventory needed (YouTube → DV360)
  • The ecosystem already in use (CM360, Google Ads → DV360 integrates)
  • The fees each DSP charges; a percent matters at crore budgets

Why ad networks still exist

A brand-new site like Vivek.com is too small for most SSPs. It joins an ad network (InMobi, Facebook Audience Network, GDN), and the network brings it to the SSP. The old price problem is gone because the exchange now sets one price by auction.

In 30 seconds

  • Inventory = ad space. You either fight for it in an auction (OA, PMP) or deal one-on-one with the publisher (PD, PG).
  • Publishers sell in this order: PG → PD → PMP → Open Auction. Leftover ("remnant") goes to OA, which is why it is cheapest.
  • Pick the cheapest method that meets the brief. Saikiran: if the bus gets you there, why book a flight?

The muskan.com example

  • OA: anyone can run ads on muskan.com.
  • PMP: only Kraftshala students can bid, but they still compete.
  • PD: only Simran and Bhavana, fixed ₹50 CPM, nothing promised.
  • PG: they pay ₹50 CPM, must buy ₹10,000 of space, and impressions are guaranteed.

Floor vs fixed price

Floor = the minimum bid in a PMP (you may bid more). Fixed = the exact price in PD/PG. Tip: start a PMP bid about 10% above floor, raise slowly if you aren't winning.

OA vs PMP in Saikiran's plan: ~£1.5 CPM bought ~13.3M impressions; a £6.5 CPM PMP bought ~6M on about double the budget. Lower CPM = more impressions for the money.

When is OA "unsafe" to promise?

OA fits almost every brief. Avoid it when the client wants committed numbers (guaranteed impressions, CTR above 1%, a 60% video completion rate). Check history first: if OA already gets ~50–55% VCR, you can optimise up to 60%; if it gets ~34%, plan a PMP.

Where PG is used

  • YouTube masthead, Times of India roadblocks
  • CTV home-screen banners, DOOH, premium apps (Uber, CRED)
  • Big launches that need high share of voice

Retargeting a first-party list? Use open auction.

Upper funnel: you don't know the user, so you buy good websites (deals) to find good users.

Lower funnel with a first-party list: you already know the user. Follow them wherever they go: Cricbuzz, Times of India, an app, YouTube. Only open auction lets you do that at low CPM.

With a deal you must wait for Khushi to visit that one publisher. Deals also add CPM on top of an already small list, and PG can't take audience targeting at all.

Use PD/PG in the lower funnel only when you have little or no first-party data. Brand safety, URL and keyword exclusions still protect you in OA.

Choosing a method in a brief: 3 checks

  1. Budget: how much can go to costly inventory?
  2. CPM range: put less where CPM is high, more where it's low and effective.
  3. Targeting & availability: can you add targeting (not on PG)? Is there enough inventory?

What do I set at each level, and how do I split it?

Why the IO exists

An extra layer for budget control. Apple wants one Black Friday awareness campaign but separate money for Mac, iPhone and TV → 3 IOs. Titan sells only watches but runs India, US and Australia → IOs by country. Split IOs wherever budgets and flights must differ.

YouTube adds a level

For YouTube: Line item → Ad group → Ad. Ad groups hold formats (skippable, non-skippable, bumper, Shorts), durations (6/15/30 s), orientations, and placement targeting.

Budget segments

A 3-month IO with Oct $1,000, Nov $2,000, Dec $1,500? Don't type $4,500 for the whole range; use Add segments to give each month its own dates and budget.

Naming convention

Campaign names look complicated on purpose: brand, market, product, format, flight, tracking code. Anyone can read what a line item is from its name.

In 30 seconds

  • Frequency = times one user sees your ad in a period. Cap it at Campaign, IO and Line item. The lowest cap wins.
  • Reach = Impressions ÷ Frequency. Raising frequency adds impressions but cuts reach.
  • Practice: broad ceiling at campaign/IO (12–15/week), real cap at line item (3–4/week).

Sensible numbers

For a consideration campaign, about 7–8 per month is a common ceiling. A team in class set 3/day at campaign level, which is 84 a month for one person: far too many. With many line items, cap at IO level so the total stays under control.

Viva: grow reach without changing budget or targeting

  1. Lower the frequency cap. After the cap is hit, the system moves on to new users.
  2. Bring CPM down. Remove costly filters you don't need for a reach goal (viewability, position targeting) so the same money buys more impressions.
  3. Broaden the audience (remove unnecessary exclusions), if the client allows it.

Line item priority

At line item level you also choose: prioritise spending my full budget, or meet a target (CPC, CPA, viewable CPM). The IO always defaults to spending its budget.

Bid shading

Stops overpaying in first-price auctions: it checks what the slot last sold for and bids just above it. Find it in Line item → Bidding → Fixed bid → Additional options. Automated bidding already does this for you.

Affinity = upper funnel

Affinity is interest only, no buying intent. Don't use it for consideration or conversion line items; use in-market, custom intent, contextual or third-party segments.

Rule of thumb

Contextual for broad awareness, audiences for consideration and bottom funnel; together is ideal. The prospecting vs remarketing split depends on the objective, how much first-party data you have, and the product.

Homework: when would you use affinity in a conversion campaign?

Rarely. Cyrus's hypothesis: only when the category is brand new and no in-market audience exists yet. For a new product in an existing category (a new phone), use in-market.

In 30 seconds

  • First-party = yours, second-party = someone's shared directly, third-party = bought from a DMP.
  • ads.txt is a public file on a publisher's site listing who may sell its inventory. It stops spoofed sites.
  • Who owns the data decides cost and quality: your own first-party list beats anything you buy.

First-party

Your visitors and customers: Floodlight data, website cookies, CRM. Used for retargeting, remarketing and lookalikes.

Second-party

Another company's data shared directly, not sold: platform audiences or a brand collaboration.

Third-party

Bought from a DMP (Truecaller, Oracle). Saikiran: last resort, after first-party and Google audiences.

Try it

Is my invalid traffic a problem?

GIVT: general invalid traffic

Known, declared bots: search crawlers, indexing and monitoring tools. Filtered automatically by platforms. Low concern; ~2–3% is normal.

SIVT: sophisticated invalid traffic

Built to cheat: fake sites, bot farms, hijacked devices. Hard to detect (mouse patterns, load times, scroll depth, CAPTCHA). Needs third-party verification.

How the fraud works

Fraudsters build a fake website with real-looking content.

They sign it up with exchanges and SSPs, so DSPs buy its ad slots.

Bots load pages and click ads, generating fake impressions and clicks.

The fraudster earns; the advertiser pays; no human ever saw the ad.

Red flag from class: 50,000 clicks but only 200 page landings. Metrics that don't relate to each other usually mean IVT.

Brand-safety levers in DV360 (line item targeting)

  • Brand suitability: exclude sensitive categories (violence, adult, politics, tragedy).
  • Apps & URLs and keywords: include or exclude lists.
  • Third-party verification: IAS or DoubleVerify for brand safety, IVT and viewability.
  • ads.txt: authorised sellers only.
  • Placement report every 1–2 weeks; exclude the bottom sites.

Viva: IVT suddenly jumps in the placement report

Identify: pull IVT and placement reports, look for sites with odd CTR, zero time on site, clicks without landings. Fix: exclude them, add verification, tighten ads.txt, move spend to PMPs.

Viva: client sees the ad on an irrelevant site

Identify: placement report for that URL, check category and context. Fix: exclude the URL/app, tighten brand-suitability categories and keyword exclusions, enable IAS/DV, share the verification report.

Which creative for which funnel stage?

Third-party creatives

Instead of uploading the file, you paste a tag (code) from an ad server like CM360: Creatives → New → Third-party display / video. The creative is hosted elsewhere; DV360 just calls it.

Why the same PG ad repeats

On PG the publisher has guaranteed your impressions, so it may show your ad again and again in one break (like the back-to-back ads on Hotstar) until the committed number is met.

Video is being skipped a lot?

  • Is there a hook in the first 5 seconds (before the skip button)?
  • Does the message match the landing page?
  • Is the CTA used, and visible in that placement?
  • Is it reaching the right audience?

Creative testing

Pull a creative-level report (Dimension: Creative + the metrics you care about). Pause what doesn't convert, ask the client for more versions of what does, and check all sizes are in use.

Great creative builds brands: Vodafone's ZooZoos, Fevicol, the Burger King mouldy-burger billboard.

In 30 seconds

  • Floodlight is a piece of code on the website that records conversions and page events (DV360's version of the Meta Pixel).
  • It gives you three things: conversion tracking, audience lists for retargeting, and signals for automated bidding.
  • Attribution decides which ad gets credit for a conversion inside the conversion window. Floodlight's default: last click, then last impression.

Activity types

  • Counter (counts events): standard (every one), unique (once per user per day), per session
  • Sales (counts revenue): transactions, items sold

Tag formats & tools

  • Image, iframe, or Google tag (gtag)
  • GTM: a container that manages all your tags in one place
  • Tag Assistant: checks whether a tag actually fires

Where it lives in DV360

Advertiser → Resources → Floodlight groups → Activities. At group level set the tag format (use dynamic) and the conversion windows. Then map Floodlight activities to line items as conversion goals.

Same idea, different names: Meta Pixel · Google Ads conversion action · DV360/CM360 Floodlight tag.

Why one KPI isn't enough

In 30 seconds

  • Two kinds of problems: delivery (under-, over- or no spend) and performance (missing KPIs like CPA, CTR, VCR).
  • Diagnose before you touch anything. Change one lever at a time, in small steps (5–20%), and note what you changed.
  • The first 3 days after launch decide pacing; don't over-react if spend is roughly on target.

The 8 optimisation levers

LeverFor a spend problemFor a performance problem
AudienceToo narrow → expandToo broad → narrow, use recency, target add-to-cart instead of page visitors
BidLow win rate → +10–20% at a time, if you have headroomAuto bidding: give it 2–3 days; check the avg CPM cap
Inventory qualityCheck deal is active on the publisher sidePlacement report, exclude bottom sites, brand safety, IVT check
FrequencyHigher cap → more spend per personLower cap → more reach, less fatigue
CreativeRejected creative = no spend; check approvals and sizesCreative report; hook in first 5 s; match landing page and funnel stage
Time & locationAdd hours or citiesPush budget into converting hours (quick commerce) and salary/festival days (Amazon, Flipkart)
Channel & deviceDon't remove a device to fix spend; it cuts spend furtherShift budget to the channel or device that performs, if it can absorb it
PacingAhead to catch up; ASAP only for short sale windowsEven for steady results on long flights

Spend vs bid trade-off: if spend is low and you still have room to raise the bid (cap ₹5, bidding ₹3), raise the bid first; it protects performance. If you are already at the cap, expand the audience instead.

Underpacing

Spending below the daily target. Causes: low bid, narrow targeting, rejected creatives, pacing/budget conflict, strict brand safety, a lower frequency cap at a higher level.

Overpacing

Usually caused by sudden last-days changes: switching to ASAP, a budget top-up, raising frequency, broad targeting, big bid jumps. Google refunds its own overspend, not yours.

No pacing

Zero spend. A paused level, creatives under review or rejected, wrong dates, or a deal not active on the publisher's SSP.

Diagnostic tools in DV360
  • Spend widget and IO table: daily spend vs target at a glance, no report needed.
  • KPI widget: goal vs actual (e.g. goal 3.78, actual 2.25 = within goal).
  • Impression-loss widget: why impressions are lost (pacing conflict, no eligible creative, frequency limited).
  • Troubleshooter (inside a line item, essential for deals): bid requests → pre-bid filters (IVT, unauthorised sellers) → targeting → creative mismatch → frequency → below floor → auction lost. Publishers ask for these screenshots.
  • Report metrics: win rate, invalid traffic, unattributed conversions, placement and creative breakdowns.
